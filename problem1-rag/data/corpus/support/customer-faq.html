Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a pilot license to fly the KX-200?

In most countries, yes, for commercial use. In the United States, commercial operators need an FAA Part 107 remote pilot certificate. Kestrel does not provide licensing, but our training partners offer a 2-day course included free with every Enterprise order.

Can the KX-200 fly in the rain?

The KX-200 is rated IP54, which means it tolerates light rain and splashing. Do not fly in heavy rain, snow or fog. Water damage from flying in heavy rain is not covered by the standard warranty.

How long is my flight data kept in Kestrel Cloud?

Telemetry and flight logs are kept for 13 months on Starter and Team plans and for 7 years on Enterprise plans. Raw payload imagery is kept for 90 days unless you move it to archive storage, which costs $0.02 per GB per month.

Can I export my data?

Yes. You can export telemetry as CSV or Parquet from the dashboard, or through the API. Payload imagery can be exported as GeoTIFF. Bulk exports larger than 500 GB are delivered as a download link valid for 7 days.

What support hours do you offer?

Standard support is available by email and chat, Monday to Friday, 08:00–18:00 US Eastern time, with a first-response target of 1 business day. Enterprise customers get 24/7 phone support with a 1-hour first-response target for critical issues.

Can I use third-party batteries?

No. The KX-200 only accepts genuine KB-22 smart batteries; it will refuse to arm with any other battery. Using modified batteries voids the warranty.

How do I update firmware?

Firmware updates are delivered over the air through the Kestrel Pilot app or the Vela gateway. Customers on the Stable channel receive updates only after they reach general availability. You can also install firmware manually from a USB drive.
